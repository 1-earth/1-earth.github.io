### Chukwu-Okoro Masks

- **Title:** The Chukwu-Okoro Masks
- **Body:** A series inspired by the socio-cultural intricacies and spiritual undertones of the Afikpo sub-village group of South-Eastern Nigeria. The works depict cultural activities, masquerades, masks, and spiritual inspirations peculiar to this region — including masks hand-carved in the early 20th century by Otu's maternal great-grandfather, the late Afikpo artist Chukwu Okoro (c.1910–1987). The series extends the telling of African stories and honours a direct line of familial craftsmanship.

### Ankara-Fairies

- **Title:** The Ankara-Fairies
- **Body:** A cosmopolitan series that combines indigenous African fabrics with painted figures of varied races and identities to represent a unifying idea of humanity. The paintings draw from Otu's life as a young man studying and living in London, where he formed lasting bonds across ethnic and cultural backgrounds. The series aims to break down cultural barriers and celebrate shared human experience.

## Artist Bio  [REVISED — expanded with PDF material]

- **Kicker:** Artist Bio
- **Title:** A practice shaped by heritage, discourse, and material experimentation.

Otu Ibe-Enwo is a Nigerian-born artist and sculptor from Afikpo in South-East Nigeria. His visual language combines surrealism and realism to create rich, layered representations that echo traditional and ancient West African art, while operating as social and philosophical commentary.

His works are multimedia — bringing together paint, textile, hair, beads, and found materials to reflect West African cultures and invite an imaginative, interactive encounter for viewers. Realistic portrayals of Afikpo masquerades, Ojude Oba festivals, Eyo festivals, and other cultural events reveal deep ties to heritage, while his compositions also explore Nsibidi philosophy, inter-cultural tolerance, gender equality, political instability, and activism.

Otu began to express his artistic intuitions at the age of three, and started sculpting with clay at seven. Though a Law graduate by formal education, he has chosen to pursue art against considerable odds — self-funding early solo exhibitions in Abuja, Enugu, and Afikpo with the support of close friends, and building a practice without the scaffolding of a formal fine-art institution.

### Lineage

Otu descends from a long line of custodians of Afikpo culture. He is the great-grandson of **Chukwu Okoro (1910–1987)**, the late sculptor, artist and wood-carver whose masks and sculptures are held internationally — including in the collections of the Smithsonian Institution, the Seattle Art Museum, and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. Chukwu Okoro's work contributed significantly to the mid-20th-century West African anthropological research of the American scholar **Professor Simon Ottenberg (1923–2023)**.

He is also the great-grandson of **Nze Nnachi Nnachi Enwo**, a warrant chief and traditional ruler (c.1880s–1930s) whose influence helped shape the Afikpo *Ogo* cultural institution.

This inherited lineage shapes the authenticity of Otu's work and continues to direct his artistic voyage.
