Artist Bio

A practice shaped by heritage, discourse, and material experimentation.

Otu Ibe-Enwo is an interesting Nigerian born artist and sculptor from Afikpo in South-East Nigeria. His visual language combines surrealism and realism to create rich, complex representations that echo traditional and ancient West African art.

His works are multimedia, often bringing together textile, hair, beads, and found materials to reflect West-African cultures and to invite an imaginative, interactive encounter for viewers.

Realistic portrayals of Afikpo masquerades, Ojude Oba festivals, Eyo festivals, and other cultural events reveal the deep ties between his work and West-African cultural heritage, while his compositions also operate as social critiques.

Otu explores Nsibidi philosophy, inter-cultural tolerance, gender inequality, political instability, and activism. His artworks blend historical, contemporary, spiritual, intellectual, and cultural analogies to forge a strong connection to his roots.

He also works within an inherited lineage of craftsmanship: his ancestor Chukwu-Okoro was a reputed artisan whose relationship with researcher Simon Ottenberg helped bring Afikpo art forms to wider global recognition.
